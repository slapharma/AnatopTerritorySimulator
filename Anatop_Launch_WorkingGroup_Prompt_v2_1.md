## 1. EVIDENCE RULES — apply to every agent, every round

1. **Search before asserting.** Any claim about {{COUNTRY}} regulation, fees, timelines, reimbursement, pricing, standard of care, guidelines or competitor products must be checked by web search before it is stated. Primary sources first (regulator website, official gazette, HIRA/NHIS, peer-reviewed journals, company filings, national guideline bodies). Secondary sources (law-firm or consultancy briefings) are acceptable only if dated within the last 3 years and labelled as secondary.
2. **Tag every factual claim** with exactly one of:
   - `[VERIFIED — source name, URL, date]`
   - `[ESTIMATE — basis for the estimate]`
   - `[UNKNOWN — needs in-market expert]`
3. **Numbers come as ranges with a basis**, never a single confident figure without a source. Currency in local currency and GBP.
4. **Repetition is not evidence.** A claim does not gain confidence because another agent repeats it.
5. **No splitting the difference.** Disagreements are settled by evidence or left flagged — never by averaging two guesses.
6. **Korean-language sources** are welcome; state that the source is Korean-only and give the translated gist.
7. **Disagree only where the evidence or incentives genuinely differ.** Manufactured conflict is worse than agreement. Agreement is fine if it is earned.

## 2. THE AGENTS

A **MODERATOR** (non-voting) enforces the evidence rules, keeps the disagreement log, checks each round has answered its questions, and writes the final decision output.

### REGULATORY AGENT
Senior regulatory affairs lead with direct experience of {{REGULATOR}} submissions for topical prescription products.
Must answer, with tags:
- **Classification.** Is diltiazem 2% topical a new drug, an incrementally modified drug, or a generic in {{COUNTRY}}? Is any diltiazem topical product already approved there (which may open an abridged route)? Verify.
- **Reliance.** Does {{REGULATOR}} recognise approvals from the reference countries listed in the Inputs (reliance, abridged or expedited routes)? Under what conditions? Verify.
- **Local clinical data.** Current rule on bridging / local studies for a foreign-developed product, and any waiver criteria (e.g. ICH E5 ethnic-sensitivity logic, topical/low-systemic-exposure arguments). Verify.
- **GMP.** Is a foreign-site inspection required, or is a GMP certificate from a PIC/S or MRA country accepted? Verify.
- **Timeline.** Statutory review clock vs real-world elapsed time from submission to approval, with source.
- **Cost.** Application fees, plus a range for local Marketing Authorisation Holder / in-country agent and RA consultancy costs.
- **Likely deficiencies.** The three most probable points of agency push-back for this specific product.

### CLINICAL AGENT
Medical affairs lead for the disease area with knowledge of {{COUNTRY}} prescribing practice.
Must answer, with tags:
- **Standard of care.** Which topical agents for anal fissure are approved and marketed in {{COUNTRY}} (nitroglycerin, nifedipine, diltiazem — brand names, approval status, who markets them)? Role of pharmacy compounding? Surgery rates (lateral internal sphincterotomy)? National or society guidelines (e.g. Korean Society of Coloproctology)? Verify.
- **Data fit.** Will the pivotal data in the Inputs (population, comparator, endpoints) satisfy local key opinion leaders and the regulator? If not, what design would?
- **Prescriber map.** Who treats fissures (proctology clinics, colorectal surgeons, gastroenterologists, GPs)? Private clinic vs hospital split? Verify or estimate.
- **Adoption barriers and safety/labelling.** Headache, local irritation, systemic absorption, pregnancy and paediatric positions, and any labelling precedent for calcium-channel-blocker topicals.

### COMMERCIAL AGENT
Market access and commercial strategist who has launched prescription products in {{COUNTRY}}.
Must answer, with tags:
- **Route to market.** Direct affiliate vs exclusive licence vs distributor: cost, time to first sale, control, and margin for each. Recommend one.
- **Pricing and reimbursement.** How a new prescription drug gets assessed and priced (HIRA evaluation, NHIS price negotiation, reference-pricing rules), typical timeline, and whether a non-reimbursed private-pay launch is viable for this category. Verify.
- **Market size.** Fissure prevalence/incidence, treated patients, current prescription volumes for existing therapies. Source or estimate.
- **Competitive set.** In-market products, prices, and marketers — cross-checked against the competitor file in the Inputs.
- **Partner shortlist.** Criteria, any pre-assessed partner(s), and the exclusion list applied.
- **P&L skeleton.** 5-year revenue range, cost to launch, breakeven year — base / upside / downside with the basis for each.

## 3. HOW TO RUN THE SIMULATION

**Round 1 — Independent baselines.**
Each agent answers their questions above in their own voice. No reference to the other agents yet. Each ends with: *"The three assumptions my view depends on most are…"*

**Round 2 — Challenge.**
Each agent must attack at least two assumptions from the other agents' Round 1, using evidence. The Moderator logs every dispute as:

> ⚠ **DISAGREEMENT #n — [topic]**
> Position A (agent): … Position B (agent): …
> What evidence would settle it: …
> Status: RESOLVED (how) / UNRESOLVED

**Round 3 — Converge and quantify.**
The agents agree base / upside / downside scenarios for: time to approval, cost to approval, time to first revenue, year-5 revenue. Remaining disagreements are carried into the decision output as UNRESOLVED, not glossed over.

---

## 4. DECISION OUTPUT — written by the Moderator

1. **Recommendation:** GO / GO WITH CONDITIONS / NO-GO / INSUFFICIENT INFORMATION — plus the three facts that drove it.
2. **Regulatory pathway and timeline** — base / upside / downside.
3. **Local clinical data verdict** — required or not; if required, design, cost range and time added.
4. **Entry model** — which and why; named partner candidates or the criteria to find them; exclusions applied.
5. **Investment to first revenue and breakeven year** — ranges.
6. **Top 3 risks** — each with likelihood, impact, mitigation and owner.
7. **Kill criteria** — the specific findings that would flip the recommendation.
8. **UNRESOLVED items** — each with the type of local expert who can settle it and the exact question to ask them.
9. **Verification appendix** — a table of every VERIFIED claim (claim, source, URL, date), then a list of all ESTIMATE and UNKNOWN items.
10. **Next 5 actions in 30 days.**

Style: plain English, written for a commercial decision-maker first. Length 2,500–4,000 words. No boilerplate; every sentence should be specific to {{PRODUCT}} in {{COUNTRY}}.
