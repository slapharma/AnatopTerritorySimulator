Anatop Territory Evaluation — User Guide

A working reference for colleagues new to the tool. It covers what the app does, how to run a session, and what everything on screen means.

What this is

A simulated cross-functional launch working group for evaluating whether — and how — to launch a pharmaceutical product in a specific country. Three AI agents (Regulatory, Clinical, Commercial) each play a specialist role, research the country live on the web, argue their case with evidence, and challenge each other's assumptions over several rounds. A fourth AI role (Moderator Assistant) synthesises the whole discussion into a structured decision document at the end.

It is not a source of truth on its own — it is a fast, structured first pass that surfaces what's known, what's estimated, what's genuinely unresolved, and where the agents disagree, each claim tagged and sourced so you can verify it yourself.

Starting a session

  1. Click New session in the sidebar.
  2. Fill in what you know. Every field is optional — anything left blank is passed to the agents as INPUT MISSING, and they are required to say what they assumed instead rather than silently guessing.
  3. Restore Default Values pre-fills the form with the saved default example (a real product, useful for a demo or a quick test). Any field you've saved as a default (see below) is included.
  4. Copy from last session pre-fills the form with whatever you entered last time.
  5. Click Start session and run Baselines. This runs all three agents in turn, each with live web search.

Note: Regulator and Reimbursement Bodies are not asked for — they follow from the Country you pick, and the agents identify them themselves as their first research step.

Saving your own defaults

Hover any field in the New Session form and a small Save as default button appears next to its label. Click it to make the field's current value the new default for everyone — the next person who clicks Restore Default Values gets it too. This is shared across the whole app, not just your browser.

The meetings

MeetingWhat happens
BaselinesEach agent answers its own questions independently, with no visibility of what the others will say.
ChallengeEach agent attacks at least two assumptions made by the others, backed by evidence — concede, rebut, or log a formal disagreement.
ConvergeAgents give base / upside / downside figures for timelines, cost, and revenue, working from the whole discussion so far.
Cross-talkOne free-form meeting: every agent reacts to the discussion so far, only where something genuinely changed their view.
Custom meeting…Write your own instruction and pick which agents respond to it.

You can also type directly into the message box at the bottom as the moderator — address it to one agent or all of them — and they'll reply.

After each of the four standard meetings finishes, the Moderator Assistant automatically writes up meeting minutes — a summary per agent plus collated action points — into the War Room panel's Minutes tab, and emails them to you with links to view the session, jump to that meeting's transcript, or approve moving to the next one.

Reading a response

Evidence badges

Every factual claim is tagged with exactly one of these:

VERIFIED checked against a real source, with a link
ESTIMATE a reasoned guess, with its basis stated
UNKNOWN needs a local in-market expert to settle

Numbered links like [1] next to a claim jump to that source in the Sources tab of the War Room panel (click War Room in the top bar to open it), which lists every URL an agent searched or cited.

Closing labels

Every response ends with at least one of these, called out as a small coloured label — the single most important takeaway of that message, not a recap of the whole thing:

Next step Question Consideration Conclusion

Cross-references

In Challenge/Converge and cross-talk responses, a mention of another agent's name (e.g. "Clinical Agent") is a link — click it to jump straight to what that agent actually said.

Disagreements

When agents genuinely disagree, it's logged as a formal disagreement — shown in the War Room's Disagreements tab as each side's position, what evidence would settle it, and a status you can toggle between UNRESOLVED and RESOLVED. "↑ view in message" jumps back to where it was raised. Disagreements are never resolved by averaging two guesses — only by evidence or an explicit toggle from you.

War Room

Click War Room in the top bar to pop out the right-hand panel: Sources, Disagreements, Cost, Minutes (auto-written meeting minutes) and Intelligence (the transcript sliced by agent, by meeting, by disagreement, or by resolution status). Close it with the ✕ or Esc.

Favourites, Regenerate, Dive Deeper

  • ☆ Favourite — star any response to flag it; filter the transcript to just your favourites with the ★ Favourites chip above the transcript.
  • ↻ Regenerate — deletes a response and has the same agent answer again from scratch. Not offered on custom-round responses, since the original instruction isn't stored.
  • ⇊ Dive Deeper — responses are compact by default. This asks the same agent to expand one specific response in full depth, as a new follow-up message, without discarding the original.

Filtering the transcript

The chip row above the transcript filters by who's speaking: an agent, You (messages you typed), Decision Output (the Moderator Assistant's synthesis — a separate thing from your own messages), or your favourites.

The model selector

Each session can use a different underlying AI model — the dropdown in the top-right of a session picks it. Free models have no per-token cost but can be slower or rate-limited; paid models cost real money (shown in the Cost tab) but tend to be faster and more reliable for testing. A change applies to turns run from that point on, not retroactively.

Cost and timing

Each response shows how long it took to generate. The topbar's ⏱ clock is a running total of generation time for the current browser tab (it resets if you reload the page — it's a convenience stopwatch, not a stored record). The Cost tab and the $ figure in the topbar are the actual API cost reported for the session, token by token.

Writing the decision output

Once you've run at least one round, Write the decision output triggers the Moderator Assistant to read the entire transcript and produce a fixed ten-section decision document — recommendation, regulatory pathway, clinical verdict, entry model, investment and breakeven, top risks, kill criteria, unresolved items, a verification appendix of every sourced claim, and next actions. It doesn't re-run the analysis or vote — it only synthesises what the three agents already established, keeping their tags and disagreements intact.

Exporting

Export ▾ in the top-right of a session produces a Word (.docx) or PDF document of the full session — inputs, every message, sources, and the decision output if one has been written.
