Anatop Territory Evaluation — User Guide

A briefing for colleagues new to the tool. It covers what the panel does, how to convene an evaluation, and what everything on screen means.

What this is

A simulated cross-functional launch board for evaluating whether — and how — to launch a pharmaceutical product in a specific country. Three AI agents — Ruth (Regulatory), Luca (Clinical) and Charlie (Commercial) — each play a specialist role, research the country live on the web, make their case on the evidence, and challenge each other's assumptions over a series of meetings. A fourth AI role (Moderator Assistant) acts as board secretary, synthesising the whole discussion into a structured decision paper at the end.

It is not a source of truth on its own — it is a fast, structured first pass that surfaces what's known, what's estimated, what's genuinely unresolved, and where the agents disagree, each claim tagged and sourced so you can verify it yourself.

The Boardroom

The Boardroom is the dashboard you land on. It carries a summary of every evaluation run so far. Starting one and listing them all aren't tiles on the dashboard — they're the New evaluation and View all evaluations buttons at the top of the sidebar, above the meeting agenda, and stay there wherever you are in the app. View all evaluations opens a page listing every evaluation in a table you can filter by title, product or country — each row shows its market, meeting progress, decision status, responses, reports, any open disagreements or escalated questions, spend (admins only), and when it was last touched. Click a row to open that evaluation. User Guide, Agents and — for admins — Admin are buttons in the header bar at the top of this and every other view, not tiles on the dashboard; on a narrower window they shrink to icons only. Clicking one opens it in the left-hand slide-over panel.

Above those sits a short How the board reaches a decision panel explaining the four steps. Once you know them it is just taking up room, so it has an ✕ to dismiss it. That choice is remembered in this browser only — it does not follow you to another machine, and clearing site data brings it back.

The Boardroom — the dashboard you land on.

Convening an evaluation

  1. Click New evaluation in the sidebar.
  2. Fill in what you know. Every field is optional — anything left blank is passed to the agents as INPUT MISSING, and they are required to say what they assumed instead rather than silently guessing.
  3. Restore Default Values pre-fills the form with the saved default example (a real product, useful for a demo or a quick test). Any field you've saved as a default (see below) is included.
  4. Copy from last session pre-fills the form with whatever you entered last time.
  5. Click Convene the panel and run Baselines. Each of the three agents presents an opening position in turn, with live web research.

Note: Regulator and Reimbursement Bodies are not asked for — they follow from the Country you pick, and the agents identify them themselves as their first research step.

The New evaluation briefing form. Every field is optional.

Saving your own defaults

Hover any field in the New evaluation briefing form and a small Save as default button appears next to its label. Click it to make the field's current value the new default for everyone — the next person who clicks Restore Default Values gets it too. This is shared across the whole app, not just your browser.

The meeting agenda

These four meetings appear in the sidebar's Meeting agenda panel as a horizontal, numbered stepper — one row of four steps, so the whole agenda fits without scrolling the sidebar: a step turns green once that meeting has run, amber if it started but stopped before every agent answered — in which case a ↻ Resume button takes its place so you can pick up where it left off. Below the stepper, Other meetings offers Custom meeting… for anything outside the standard four — this is also where you reach Autopilot, see below — and a Stop after this turn button appears while a meeting is running.

MeetingWhat happens
BaselinesOpening positions. Each agent presents an independent assessment, with no visibility of what the others will say.
ChallengeScrutiny. Each agent tests at least two assumptions made by the others, backed by evidence — concede, rebut, or log a formal disagreement.
ConvergeConsensus. The panel tables base / upside / downside figures for timelines, cost, and revenue, working from the whole discussion so far.
Cross-talkOpen floor. Every agent reacts to the debate so far, only where something genuinely changed their view.
Custom meeting…Table your own agenda item and pick which agents respond to it, once or on Autopilot.

You can also type directly into the message box at the bottom as the moderator — address it to one agent or all of them, by name (“Ruth, what would the deficiency letter say?”) — and they'll reply.

After each of the four standard meetings finishes, the Moderator Assistant automatically writes up meeting minutes — a summary per agent plus collated action points — into the Minutes tab of the Intelligence page, and emails them to you with links to view the session, jump to that meeting's transcript, or approve moving to the next one.

Autopilot

Autopilot has no button of its own — it's a mode of the Custom meeting… dialog. Click Custom meeting…, write the agenda item (this is what the panel will debate), and pick which agents take part. Under How it runs, choose Once — each picked agent responds a single time, the same as any other custom meeting — or Autopilot, which lets the panel debate the agenda item over several cycles unaided, within limits you set: response length, the number of interactions (cycles), whether it stops early once every agent agrees, and a stance slider per agent from supportive through to adversarial, so you can weight how hard each one pushes back. The button label switches from Convene meeting to Start autopilot once Autopilot is selected.

Autopilot on this disagreement…, offered from a disagreement's own dialog (see Disagreements, below), opens this same Custom meeting dialog — preset to Autopilot on that disagreement, with the agenda item and run mode already chosen for you and an extra Auto-resolve this disagreement when unanimous option.

Reading a response

Evidence badges

Every factual claim is tagged with exactly one of these:

VERIFIED checked against a real source, with a link
ESTIMATE a reasoned guess, with its basis stated
UNKNOWN needs a local in-market expert to settle

Numbered links like [1] next to a claim open the Sources tab of the Intelligence page and jump straight to that source, which lists every URL an agent searched or cited.

A live evaluation, with the three-column transcript, the meeting agenda stepper and Intelligence navigation in the sidebar, and VERIFIED / ESTIMATE badges inline.

Closing labels

Every response ends with at least one of these, called out as a small coloured label — the single most important takeaway of that message, not a recap of the whole thing:

Next step Question Consideration Conclusion

Cross-references

In Challenge/Converge and cross-talk responses, a mention of another agent's name (e.g. "Luca") is a link — click it to jump straight to what that agent actually said.

Disagreements

When agents genuinely disagree, it's logged as a formal disagreement — shown in the Intelligence page's Disagreements tab (under Panel) as each side's position, what evidence would settle it, and a status you can toggle between UNRESOLVED and RESOLVED. "↑ view in message" jumps back to where it was raised (and switches you back to the transcript to show it). Disagreements are never resolved by averaging two guesses — only by evidence or an explicit toggle from you.

Opening a disagreement offers Ask selected agents to put a question or steer to whichever agents you tick, and Autopilot on this disagreement…, which opens the Custom meeting dialog preset to let the panel debate it unaided (see Autopilot, above).

The Disagreements tab — each side's position, what would settle it, and its status.

Agent Questions

When an agent ends a response with a "Questions for …" block, each question in it is picked up automatically and listed in the Intelligence page's Agent Questions tab (under Panel) — who asked whom, which meeting it was raised in (the message link opens it), and a status: Open, Answered, Resolved or Escalated to moderator. The tab's count is open-plus-escalated over the total; filter the list to Open, Escalated, Answered or All. For an evaluation run before this feature existed, Find questions in this evaluation reads the whole transcript for them; once any are found, the same button moves into the filter bar as Rescan transcript.

After each meeting completes, the Moderator Assistant re-reads the transcript and marks any open question that a later message has already answered, noting who answered and linking to it. The filter bar's Check for answers now runs the same check on demand, next to Rescan transcript. It only looks at questions still Open, so anything you've settled by hand is left alone.

The Agent Questions tab, filtered to Open — each card carries its own View and downloads alongside the actions below.

  • Answer… — write the answer yourself. It is posted into the transcript addressed to the asker, the question is marked Answered, and you can optionally ask the asker to respond.
  • Ask agents to answer… — tick one or more agents; each answers the question in turn, posted into the transcript as a custom turn, and once they're done the Moderator Assistant checks whether that answered it.
  • Discuss to resolution… (only for a question put to another agent) — each loop, the agent(s) asked answer and then the asker says whether that settles it. It ends Resolved once the asker is satisfied, or Escalated to moderator for offline review if it's still unsettled after the number of loops you set (default 3, up to 10) or the autopilot cost cap is hit; stopping the run, or a failed turn, leaves it Open instead. It runs on the Autopilot engine and appears in the transcript as a "Question discussion", with a loop header per round.
  • Mark as Resolved sets the question straight to Resolved, Escalate to Moderator sets it to Escalated to moderator, and Reopen puts it back to Open — all by hand, at any point.

Every escalated question, however it got there, also collects in its own Escalations tab, with the same Answer…, Discuss to resolution… and Reopen actions as the Agent Questions cards. Its count turns amber while anything is waiting.

Besides the automatic meeting minutes, an entry is added to Minutes every time a question is worked on — answered by the moderator, discussed to resolution (any outcome, including stopped or a failed turn), marked answered, escalated or reopened by hand, or found already answered by the automatic check. Each is a plain record: who asked whom, the question, what happened, the status change, and for a discussion the loops, who contributed with their message numbers, and the asker's verdict. These entries aren't emailed and have no Approve button — "view in transcript" jumps to the relevant message.

Intelligence

Intelligence is a page of the evaluation, shown in place of the transcript rather than a pop-out panel. It has no button of its own in the header — you reach it from the sidebar, in an Intelligence section below Meeting agenda, laid out as a vertical navigation grouped under four headings: Meeting transcript on its own (switches back to the live conversation); Evidence — Sources, Knowledgebase; Panel — Agent notes, Agent Questions, Escalations, Disagreements; Outcome — Decision & reports (the recommendation, once one has been written, plus every generated report), Minutes (auto-written meeting minutes and question activity — an unapproved Baselines, Challenge or Converge entry has an Approve and continue button that approves it and convenes the next meeting on the agenda; Cross-talk minutes get a plain Approve instead, since there's nothing after it to convene), Favourites; and Briefing — Inputs. Agent notes slices the transcript by agent, by meeting, by disagreement, or by resolution status — the By Meeting cut names each Autopilot conversation for what it is, e.g. "Question discussion: Ruth → Luca", "Disagreement debate #2", or "Panel debate 1" for an open debate, rather than lumping them together as "Autopilot". On a narrow window, where the sidebar is hidden, a Show picker in the evaluation's header bar switches between the transcript and each tab instead. There's no ✕ or Esc to close a tab: starting any agent turn — a meeting, Autopilot, a discussion, a reply — switches you straight back to the transcript to show it running, and so do "view in message"/"view in transcript" links from any tab.

Knowledgebase

The Knowledgebase tab (under Evidence) holds curated internal documents — Drive links, web pages — that every agent is told about at the start of every turn, in every evaluation; it's shared across the whole app, not scoped to the one you're looking at. Anyone can view it. Admins get a form at the top of the tab to add a source (URL, title, category, a note on what it covers, and whether it's commercially sensitive — pricing, royalties, deal terms), and Edit / Delete buttons on each row of the table below.

Favourites, Regenerate, Dive Deeper

  • ☆ Favourite — star any response to flag it. Starred responses are collected in the Favourites tab of the Intelligence page, where each one can be opened, jumped to in the transcript, or unstarred.
  • ↻ Regenerate — deletes a response and has the same agent answer again from scratch. Not offered on custom-round responses, since the original instruction isn't stored.
  • ⇊ Dive Deeper — responses are compact by default. This asks the same agent to expand one specific response in full depth, as a new follow-up message, without discarding the original.

Reading the transcript

Agent responses are laid out in three columns — Ruth (Regulatory), Luca (Clinical), Charlie (Commercial) — always in that order, so a column position means the same agent everywhere on the page. Each column is headed by a colour-tinted band naming the agent, with their headshot next to it. An agent that did not speak in a round leaves its column empty rather than shifting the others along. Responses are grouped by meeting: each round is one aligned row across the three columns, under a divider naming it.

Choosing and switching the model

Each evaluation can run on a different underlying AI model. You pick the starting model under Meeting settings at the bottom of the New evaluation briefing form. You can switch it later from Model at the top of the Intelligence page's Inputs tab, between meetings (it is locked while a meeting is running). A switch applies to turns from then on; earlier responses keep the model they were written on, and a Model change note is added to the transcript (shown as System (model) in exports) so the record shows where it happened. The default — used automatically if you don't change it, for every user — is Mistral Nemo, a low-cost paid model (see Cost and timing, below); no admin action is needed to be on it. The free models (Gemma, Nemotron) cost nothing per token but are rate-limited, so they're there as a fallback rather than the default. Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-OSS 20B and Qwen 3.7 Flash are also paid, and picking one of those still requires an administrator — anyone else who tries gets an error. Claude Sonnet 5 is the strongest of the three and by far the most expensive, so reach for it when a run has to stand up rather than for routine testing.

Cost and timing

Each response shows how long it took to generate. The topbar's ⏱ clock is a running total of generation time for the current browser tab (it resets if you reload the page — it's a convenience stopwatch, not a stored record). The $ · £ figure in the topbar is a button: click it to open LLM usage and cost for the open session — summary tiles (total cost, model calls including failed ones, API requests, tokens in/out, web searches), then a breakdown By use (Agent presentations, Question resolution, Disagreement resolution, Autopilot discussion, Reports and decision, Meeting minutes, each expandable into its features, e.g. Baselines, Answered check, Question discussion, Disagreement discussion, Interim/Final report), By model and By agent, and a list of every individual call with its time, use, feature, agent, model, tokens, cost and status.

LLM usage and cost, opened from the $ · £ figure in the topbar.

The total counts every model call, including ones that leave no message in the transcript — the questions answered-check, meeting minutes, an interim report, or a turn that failed part-way (at whatever it had spent before failing) — so it can run higher than the sum of the costs shown on individual responses. Calls made before this per-call tracking existed are read back out of the transcript and marked transcript. Costs are what OpenRouter reported for each request; £ figures use the configured exchange rate and are estimates. Cost is shown to admins only. The Cost section of the Admin page still has the across-session view.

The board recommendation

Once you've run at least one round, the Final report… button — sitting under the decision output on the Intelligence page's Decision & reports tab — triggers the Moderator Assistant to read the entire transcript and produce a fixed eleven-section decision document — the recommendation, the regulatory pathway, whether local clinical data is needed, how we would enter the market, the reconciled numbers, where the agents disagreed, the top risks, what would change the decision, what is still unresolved, an evidence table of every sourced claim, and the next five actions. It doesn't re-run the analysis or vote — it only synthesises what the three agents already established, keeping their tags and disagreements intact.

The result lands in two places: as a Moderator Assistant message in the transcript, and as a card in the report list on the same Decision & reports tab, alongside every other report generated for the evaluation — each with View, Word, PDF, Excel, Email… and Delete. An Interim report… button next to it works the same way at any earlier point, but does not write a decision.

Decision & reports — every report generated for the evaluation, each with its own View and downloads.

Viewing and downloading

There is no Export menu. Instead, each thing you might want to keep opens on its own with View and downloads as Word, PDF or Excel: every report, the decision output, each minutes entry, every response (open it with ⤢ in the transcript, from Agent notes or from Favourites), each disagreement (in its dialog) and each agent question. On the Minutes tab, every Baselines, Challenge, Converge and Cross-talk entry also offers the Full transcript of that meeting — every response in it, in order — with the same View and downloads. In the Excel files, a meeting transcript is one row per response (number, speaker, meeting, time, response); anything else is one row per heading, paragraph or bullet with the section it sits under, and tables inside it keep their own columns.

Minutes — every entry, and every standard meeting's full transcript, downloads on its own.
